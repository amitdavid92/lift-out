# Two-minute elevator pitch

About 300 words, which runs close to two minutes at a normal speaking pace.

## Key takeaway

Web pages make you cope with everything at once. Lift Out lets you take one piece, pull it out of the noise, and understand it.

## Some details

It's a Chrome extension. Hold Alt (Option on a Mac), click any block on any page, and three things happen. The block lifts off the page into a clean, enlarged, high-contrast panel, with the ads, sidebars and pop-ups gone. Beside it, a plain-language explanation writes itself: what this thing is, what it says, and what any jargon means. Below it, cards fan out showing the other parts of the page it connects to, such as the table behind the claim or the footnote it cites. Click one and it swaps in. Press Escape and everything goes back where it was. All of it works from the keyboard and with a screen reader, and it respects reduced-motion settings.

## Obviously it isn't yet…

…a finished product. It's a one-day prototype. It needs your own API key, it only finds related content on the same page, and although it sends images to the model to be described, it doesn't yet write that description back into the page as alt text. It hasn't been tested with real users of assistive technology, and that's the test that matters.

## Why use it

Because it takes one click and stays out of your way. If you have low vision, dyslexia or ADHD, or you're reading in a second language, or you're just tired and facing a wall of legal text, you don't need a different website. You need this paragraph, bigger, clearer and explained.

## The impact on understanding

On our test page, a sentence about "a ring-fenced levy on commercial ratepayers, subject to the capex ceiling" became "businesses in the area pay an extra charge, and there's a cap on building costs", with the footnote defining that cap one click away. Understanding it stops depending on how well you can fight the page.

## Live demo to go with it

1. Open the JLL market commentary page (see `ideas.md` for links).
2. Hold Alt and sweep the mouse so the outline is seen following the cursor.
3. Click a dense paragraph and let the description stream in.
4. Click one related card to show the swap.
5. Press Escape so the block settles back into place.
